CHAPTER 1

Inside and outside functions

Many functions are one function placed inside another. Before you can differentiate them, you need to see which part is which.

THE IDEA
The inside function is the part you would work out first. The outside function is what you do to it afterwards.

Take y = (x² + 1)³ as an example: to find y for a number x, you first work out x² + 1, then cube the result. So x² + 1 is the inside function and cubing is the outside function. A quick test: cover the brackets with your thumb. What your thumb hides is the inside; what is left showing is the outside.

Why it matters. When you differentiate a function inside a function (Chapter 3), the inside changes too, and that change has to be counted. You can only count it if you have spotted the inside function first.

The power rule

The derivative dy/dx tells you how fast y changes as x changes. On a graph, it is the slope of the curve. For powers of x there is one rule that does almost all the work.

THE POWER RULE
Bring the power down in front, then lower the power by one.
If y = xⁿ, then dy/dx = n·xⁿ⁻¹
NUMBERS IN FRONTA number multiplying x stays in front.
5x³ → 15x²
ONE TERM AT A TIMEDifferentiate a sum one term at a time.
x² + x → 2x + 1
PLAIN NUMBERSThe derivative of a constant number is 0.
7 → 0
WORKED EXAMPLE · y = 4x³ − 5x + 2
First term: power rule4x³ → 4 · 3x² = 12x²
Second term: x is x¹−5x → −5
Plain number+2 → 0
Put it togetherdy/dx = 12x² − 5
Watch out. Lower the power by exactly one: x⁴ becomes 4x³, not 4x⁴ and not 4x². And x on its own has power one, so its derivative is 1.

CHAPTER 3

The chain rule

Use the chain rule when one function sits inside another, like (x² + 1)³ or √(x + 5). Start by naming the inside and outside functions, as in Chapter 1.

THE CHAIN RULE
Differentiate the outside, keeping the inside as it is. Then multiply by the derivative of the inside.
dy/dx = (derivative of outside) × (derivative of inside)
WORKED EXAMPLE · y = (x³ + 2)⁴
Name the partsinside: x³ + 2  ·  outside: ( )⁴
Outside, inside kept4(x³ + 2)³
Derivative of inside3x²
Multiply themdy/dx = 4(x³ + 2)³ · 3x²
Tidy updy/dx = 12x²(x³ + 2)³
The most common slip: stopping after the outside. Using the power rule on the brackets is only half the job. The inside is changing too, so you must multiply by the derivative of the inside.

Check yourself: if the inside is just x, its derivative is 1 and the chain rule changes nothing. That is why the plain power rule works for x⁵ on its own, while (3x − 2)⁵ needs the extra factor.

CHAPTER 4

Slopes and turning points

A derivative is a slope you can calculate. Put a number in for x and you get the steepness of the curve at that point.

SLOPE AT A POINT
To find the slope at a point, work out dy/dx, then substitute the x value of that point.
TURNING POINTS
At a turning point the curve is flat, so the slope is zero: set dy/dx = 0 and solve for x.
WORKED EXAMPLE · turning point of y = x² − 6x + 5
Differentiatedy/dx = 2x − 6
Slope is zero2x − 6 = 0
Add 6 to both sides2x = 6
Divide both sides by 2x = 3
Find y therey = 9 − 18 + 5 = −4, so the point is (3, −4)
Solving dy/dx = 0. Undo one operation at a time, and whatever you do to one side, do to the other. Moving a number across the equals sign changes its sign: adding becomes subtracting.
